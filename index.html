My PS5 agent: Post-Purchase Feedback Sorter

My agent card | Post-Purchase Feedback Sorter | finished 20 Oct 2026, 9:51 PM

  1. The job: Sorts each new post-purchase feedback response into a theme and sentiment using keyword rules in Google Sheets, then emails me a labelled summary, for an online shop’s customer experience manager.
  2. The trigger: A new row in the responses sheet of my Google Form “Post-Purchase Feedback (test)”.
  3. The tools: Google Sheets (New Spreadsheet Row, reads the response and its labels) and Gmail (Send Email, to my own address only).
  4. It must never: Email anyone but me; the Gmail “To” field is typed as my own address, with no mapped fields.
  5. Where a person checks: I read every labelled email each evening and personally follow up any response that needs a reply or is marked “Unclear – needs review” before results are shared.

How it works

Zapier free plan, one trigger and one action. Google Forms writes each response to a Google Sheet. Two formulas in the sheet label it: a keyword rule sets the category, and the 1–5 rating sets the sentiment (4–5 Positive, 3 Mixed, 1–2 Negative). Zapier picks up the new row and emails me the labels. AI by Zapier is not available on the free plan, so the sorting is done by rules I wrote, not by a language model. All responses are test data I wrote myself.

The tricky case and the fix

What I expected before sending it: the comment contains “arrived”, so I expected the keyword rules to label it Delivery & shipping. That is the wrong outcome: it is a customer asking for help, so a person must answer it, and in a weekly theme report it would be counted and never replied to.

The fix: I added a rule that is checked first: any comment with a question mark, “where is”, “never arrived”, “no reply” or “nobody replies” is labelled Needs a reply – person to handle. I then submitted the identical response again.

Q1. Where my agent sits on the map

(a) Quadrant: AI alone, with checks.

(b) The two questions. Is there a right answer? Yes, for my build. I fixed the seven categories in advance, so every comment has one correct label under the rules, and I could mark each run right or wrong: three of four first-round runs matched what I expected, and the miss (the “where is my order?” case) had a clearly better label. Must people and the agent do it together, or can either do it alone? Either alone. All five runs happened with no one in the loop; Zapier read each row, used the labels and emailed me within seconds. A person could also do the same sorting alone, just more slowly. People come in afterwards, as a check.

(c) The human role. The agent may label responses and email them to me on its own. A person checks every evening: I read the labelled emails, personally answer anything marked Needs a reply, and judge anything marked Unclear – needs review before results are shared. My runs show why that check matters: the rules read words, not meaning. “Good expirence” and “I don’t have” both fell to Unclear, and the support request was filed as a delivery theme until I added a rule. The agent must never contact customers. Two changes would move it. If it replied to customers itself, its mistakes would reach them, so a person would need to lead and approve each reply (A person leads, AI inside). If an AI found the themes itself instead of my fixed list, there would be no single right grouping, and it would belong in Agents run it, a person judges.

(d) Our group’s placement. Our group put “Analyse and categorise Google Forms feedback” in Agents run it, a person judges, on the view that there is no single right way to group open-ended feedback. I agree for the task we imagined, where AI discovers the themes. Building it changed my answer to the first question for my version: because a person set the categories first, the task now has a right answer, and the agent can run alone with checks. So I would answer the right-answer question differently for a fixed-category agent, and keep our group’s placement for the theme-discovery version.

Collaboration log

What I delegated to AI: I used Claude (an AI assistant) to plan the build, write the keyword and sentiment formulas, draft the agent card, and draft this page and the first version of Q1, which I reviewed and edited. The feedback sorting itself is done by keyword rules, not by AI.

How I checked it: I wrote the expected label for every test response before submitting it, then compared each email with its run in Zap history and its row in the sheet.

One AI error I caught: Claude’s first design used AI by Zapier for the categorising. When I tried to add it, Zapier showed it was not available on the free plan, so I asked for a free-plan design and switched to keyword rules in Google Sheets. Claude’s first formula paste also failed with #ERROR! because it was split over several lines; the one-line version worked.

Time and cost: about 2 hours on 20 Oct 2026; S$0 (Zapier free plan, Google Forms, Sheets and Gmail).

Reflection

The agent fitted the job well for clear, single-topic comments: all three normal runs were labelled correctly within seconds of each other. It failed where meaning mattered more than keywords, filing an urgent “where is my order?” request as a delivery theme. Next time I would add a step on a paid plan that saves labels to a tracker and alerts me immediately for anything that needs a reply, and I would review a sample of Unclear responses to grow the keyword list.
